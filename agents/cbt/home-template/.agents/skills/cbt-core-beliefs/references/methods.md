# Belief and perfectionism methods

## Downward arrow

Capture one automatic thought, then ask what it would mean to the user if it were true and why it matters. Follow one answer at a time. A few links often suffice; stop when further questioning adds nothing or becomes overwhelming. Return from the conditional exploration to examining whether the resulting rule is justified and useful. Record the user's meanings separately from any agent hypothesis.

## Book domains

- Approval: self-evaluation depends heavily on others' judgments.
- Love: happiness or worth is made conditional on being loved/partnered.
- Achievement: output/status is treated as the measure of the person.
- Perfectionism: anything short of an extreme standard is taken as failure.
- Entitlement: a wish or perceived merit becomes a guarantee others must deliver.
- Omnipotence: responsibility is assumed for others' feelings or choices beyond actual influence.
- Autonomy: the historical scale's beliefs about mood/control. Discuss carefully; biological, social and environmental constraints remain real.

These are possible rules to explore, not labels or diagnoses. A domain may contain both realistic concerns and an overly rigid inference.

## Methods

**Costs/benefits:** compare the rule and a more flexible version, including what the user fears losing. Preserve values such as care, skill and connection while reconsidering an all-or-nothing condition.

**Approval/love experiments:** predict an outcome of a small safe disagreement, independent enjoyable activity or respectful request; observe both the external response and the user's capacity to cope. Do not encourage breaking important relationships to “prove independence.”

**Achievement/self-worth:** examine standards across different people and life stages; diversify sources of meaning and observe whether worthwhile experience exists outside measured productivity.

**Perfectionism experiments:** compare reasonable effort levels on a low-stakes task; set a realistic time boundary; reduce one redundant check; count what worked as well as errors; choose process goals rather than guaranteed outcomes. A deliberate small imperfection is optional and only safe in an appropriate context.

**Role reversal/acceptance:** rehearse responding to an imperfect result without global self-condemnation. Do not turn acceptance into approving preventable harm or abandoning the user's real aims.

**Revised rule:** write a credible flexible statement, situations where it applies, and what would change one's mind. Revisit through observed behavior rather than endless affirmation.
