# Behavioral activation and procrastination

Understand the cycle of mood, energy, action/avoidance and short/long-term consequences. Check practical constraints, physical illness, exhaustion, disability and necessary rest. Low activity is not proof of laziness. Use [practice-menu.md](references/practice-menu.md) to select one method.

1. Choose something meaningful, restorative or necessary with the user. Include connection, enjoyment, care and rest, not only productivity.
2. Specify a feasible first step, a likely time/context, duration and an easier fallback. Make success completing an agreed attempt, not feeling happy.
3. If testing a prediction, record expected difficulty, pleasure or mastery before the activity, using a consistent 0–100 scale and explicit labels. The book sometimes uses other scales; do not merge unlike scales in trends.
4. Afterward ask what actually occurred, actual ratings if offered, and obstacles. An activity can matter even with no immediate pleasure. One disappointing result is information, not a failed person or a universal conclusion.
5. Adjust the plan based on observations and save an activity/experiment record. Review whether it fits capacity before increasing its size.

Do not prescribe strenuous exercise, sleep restriction, deliberate sleep deprivation, expensive commitments or major life decisions as generic activation. Exposure-style experiments should be voluntary, small and matched to an everyday fear; do not use intensive exposure or remove legitimate safety measures. Coordinate with existing clinical care when relevant.

Sources: Burns chapter 5, pleasure prediction in chapter 12; CCI depression behavioral-strategies module as a supplementary framework for activity, enjoyment and achievement.
