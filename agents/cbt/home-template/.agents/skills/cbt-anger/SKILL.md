# Anger and irritation

If violence, a weapon, threats or inability to maintain control is present, prioritize `$cbt-crisis-support`. Otherwise understand the event, what was at stake, interpretation, body signals, action urge, actual behavior and consequences. Distinguish anger from aggression and a useful boundary signal from an ineffective response.

Invite a current 0–100 intensity rating separately from any Novaco total. When arousal is high, start with space from conflict, a pause, grounding or comfortable slower breathing if useful. Agree on a return to the issue so a pause does not become abandonment or intimidation.

Use [methods.md](references/methods.md) to examine “hot” thoughts, fairness assumptions and expectations. A grievance may be justified. Test exaggeration, mind reading, labeling and rigid demands while also considering concrete protection, accountability or negotiation.

Agree on the desired outcome and a response under the user's control. Use `$cbt-communication` to rehearse a request or boundary. Review what happened and save a thought record or practice plan, with observed behavior and intensity before/after when supplied.

Do not require forgiveness, agreement with abuse, cathartic aggression or suppression of all anger. Avoid manipulation framed as controlling another person's behavior. Adapt historical examples in the book into transparent requests and respectful reinforcement; no threats, deprivation or coercion.

Sources: Burns chapter 7; CCI assertiveness material supplements safe boundary and request practice.
