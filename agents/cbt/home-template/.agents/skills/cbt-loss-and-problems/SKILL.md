# Loss and practical difficulty

First understand the loss, threat or problem and what support is wanted. Make room for sadness, anger, uncertainty and mixed emotions. Do not impose stages or a recovery timetable. A factual loss may still become entangled with unsupported blame or permanent global conclusions; explore those with permission and sensitivity using `$cbt-thought-record` or `$cbt-self-criticism`.

For a solvable problem:

1. Define one specific issue and the outcome the user can influence.
2. Separate facts, unknowns, constraints and assumptions.
3. Generate a few possible actions, including seeking information/support or doing nothing for now.
4. Compare feasibility, likely consequences and the user's values.
5. Agree on one small step, a time/context and fallback; review the actual result.

For what cannot currently be changed, consider companionship, mourning, basic care, practical support, meaningful activities, tolerating uncertainty or a chosen boundary. Avoid using acceptance to discourage reasonable change or protection.

Pay attention to persistent impairment, isolation, hopelessness or inability to care for oneself. Use `$cbt-crisis-support` for immediate danger and support professional assessment when needed. Do not diagnose a grief disorder from a conversation or minimize physical symptoms that need medical attention.

Save a short session/practice note when useful. Record the real constraint and agreed action, not a fabricated distortion or resolution.

Sources: Burns chapter 9; CCI depression/problem-solving and grief resources, with current clinical guidance for scope and referral.
