# Choosing a method

Choose with the user; this table offers hypotheses, not a diagnostic classifier.

| Presenting need | Useful first direction | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| A painful concrete interpretation | Thought record | What supports it, what may be missing, what action follows if it is true? |
| Low energy, avoidance, loss of reinforcement | Behavioral activation | Energy/health constraints, rest needs, task size and actual reward |
| Global self-attack, shame or guilt | Self-criticism work | Specific behavior versus personal worth; realistic responsibility |
| Anger and escalating conflict | Anger work | Immediate safety, boundary violation, goals and physiological arousal |
| Criticism or difficulty saying no | Communication practice | Power imbalance, safety and the desired boundary |
| Repeated rule across situations | Core-belief work | Timing, readiness and whether a testable rule is more useful than more introspection |
| Bereavement or real adversity | Grief support/problem solving | Space to feel, concrete support, controllable versus uncontrollable aspects |
| Rumination with no new information | Shift to action or attention | Is another discussion adding evidence or repeating reassurance? |
| Intense distress with poor concentration | Brief stabilization and companionship | Safety, orienting to the room, comfortable breathing, nearby support |
| Current intent, means or inability to stay safe | Crisis support | Immediate help takes priority over all exercises |

An emotion is not a cognitive distortion. A thought can be accurate and still too global, inflexible or unhelpful in a particular form. Challenge the inference, not the user's right to feel. Conversely, do not avoid cognitive inquiry merely because the situation really is difficult.

For ambivalence, explore what the emotion/rule protects, its costs, and the degree of change the user wants. This is an optional adaptation of TEAM agenda-setting, not a claim that resistance explains every difficulty. Avoid pressure to recover quickly or agree with the agent.

When a method stalls, first ask whether you understood the concern and whether the method fits. Offer another angle, a smaller action, or a pause. Do not keep asking the same counterevidence question in different words until the user gives in.
