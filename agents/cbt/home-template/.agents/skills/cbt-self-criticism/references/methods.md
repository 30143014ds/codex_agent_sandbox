# Methods for self-attack and guilt

- **Three columns:** capture the attack, a possible cognitive error, and an accurate response. Replace “I am useless” with a specific account of what happened, what remains unknown and what can be done.
- **Common standard:** ask how the user would judge another person in comparable circumstances; use the same evidence and responsibilities for both.
- **Specificity and continuum:** define the criticized behavior, degree and context instead of debating an entire identity.
- **Contribution map:** list causal factors and the user's actual control, information and intent at the time. Percentage allocations are optional approximations, not objective proof. Do not use them to excuse harm or blame victims.
- **Remorse into repair:** identify harm, an achievable apology/correction/restitution where safe, learning and a limit to repetitive punishment. Respect the other person's choice about contact or forgiveness.
- **Should-language review:** distinguish an ethical commitment from an impossible demand or hindsight claim. Translate it into a chosen standard and a practical next action.
- **Achievement log:** count concrete efforts and actions to counter selective attention. Do not make worth contingent on the log.
- **Cost/benefit of self-criticism:** ask whether the current tone actually supports the value it claims to protect. Try a firm, fair coaching response and compare its effect.

For shame after a real mistake, challenge a global identity conclusion while retaining accountability. For guilt over uncontrollable events, examine the chain of causation rather than offering unsupported certainty that “nothing was your fault.” For loss, allow sadness and use `$cbt-loss-and-problems` as needed.
