# Chapter 14 practice coverage

This menu preserves the scope of Burns' fourteen suggestions, using brief original summaries rather than copied case narratives. Select only a fitting practice. Safety and user choice override a literal enactment of a historical example.

1. Compare the costs and benefits of perfectionistic rules.
2. Test whether different reasonable standards change output and effectiveness.
3. Compare perfection ratings with enjoyment across ordinary activities.
4. Examine whether an absolute perfection criterion is definable or achievable.
5. Identify a feared consequence and try a small, safe imperfection if wanted.
6. Use downward-arrow inquiry to identify the rule behind the fear.
7. Set process goals within one's control rather than guaranteed outcomes.
8. Try sensible time limits to reduce endless polishing.
9. Write a brief personally credible reflection on mistakes and learning.
10. Count actual constructive actions instead of attending only to errors.
11. Examine all-or-nothing judgments against degrees and mixed evidence.
12. With a safe person, admit an ordinary uncertainty or ask for feedback; no forced disclosure.
13. Recall a meaningful enjoyable experience and examine whether perfection made it meaningful; skip evocative imagery if unwanted.
14. Compare total useful output under reasonable standards with a single endlessly polished product; retain genuine quality requirements.

Items 2–5, 8, 12 and 14 can use the workspace experiment form. Items 1, 6, 7 and 9–13 can use a thought record or short session/practice note. Do not assume lower standards are always better, especially in safety-critical tasks.
