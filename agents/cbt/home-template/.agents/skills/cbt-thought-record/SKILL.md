# Thought record

Use [methods.md](references/methods.md) for the distortion taxonomy and alternative methods. Start with one concrete situation and one emotionally important thought. An accurate appraisal, a mixed appraisal and uncertainty are all legitimate outcomes.

1. Establish what happened and what the user made of it; do not rewrite their experience into an agent-authored story.
2. Capture the automatic thought in the user's words. Invite belief strength 0–100 and emotion names/intensities 0–100. Bodily sensations and behavior can clarify the cycle but are optional.
3. Explore evidence supporting the thought fairly before looking for missing facts, alternatives and evidence against it. Ask useful questions, not a courtroom cross-examination. One concrete counterexample can qualify a universal claim but does not prove a specific situation is safe or pleasant.
4. If useful, identify one or two possible distortion categories with the user. Record tentative versus agreed classifications. Do not classify an emotion or every unpleasant statement as a distortion.
5. Help formulate a response the user finds credible. It can acknowledge a painful fact and reject an unsupported conclusion, or state uncertainty and a practical next step. A response need not be positive.
6. Invite belief/emotion reratings; accept no change, worsening or a declined rating. Ask what was learned and whether a safe behavioral experiment would add useful evidence.
7. Save a thought record using `$cbt-notes`, including unknown/skipped fields and the next question if unfinished.

For a brief form use Burns' three columns: automatic thought, possible thinking error, realistic response. For more complex work add evidence for/against, action and ratings. Do not force every field into every exchange.

If evidence supports a threat or mistreatment, combine coping/boundary/problem-solving work with any relevant secondary cognition. If the user cannot identify thoughts, explore a recent moment, an image or meaning gently, or switch to behavior/support. Avoid repeated reassurance loops and forced vivid imagery of traumatic events.

Sources: Burns chapters 3–4 and related exercises; Beck guided discovery. The workspace thought-record schema includes explicit uncertainty and the book's ten-category taxonomy.
