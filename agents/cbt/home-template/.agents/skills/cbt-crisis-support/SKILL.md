# Immediate support and safety

Pause routine cognitive challenges and full questionnaires. Respond directly and calmly to the specific concern. Be present without claiming physical presence, continuous monitoring or an emergency-service role.

## Establish what is happening now

Ask short direct questions appropriate to what is already known: is there immediate danger, have they already acted or taken a substance, is there current intent/plan or access to means, and is a safe supportive person nearby? Ask only what helps the next action; do not pursue graphic details. Direct positive depression safety items need follow-up even when other items are missing. Passive thoughts, active intent and imminent action require different responses; clarify rather than assuming they are equivalent.

## Act on immediate danger

If an attempt, poisoning/overdose, current intent with access to means, serious threat to someone, or inability to stay safe is apparent, encourage local emergency help now and help the user contact a trusted nearby person who can stay with them or assist. Suggest moving away from means or placing them out of reach with another person's help when feasible and safe. With danger to another person, encourage separation and emergency support; do not advise confrontation or handling a weapon.

Do not wait for a score, a long intake or a web lookup before this advice. Ask location only to connect to appropriate help; never infer country from language. Before giving a specific crisis number, use a reliable available contact tool or verify an official local source. If verification is unavailable, say so and refer to local emergency services/known existing care instead of inventing a number. Do not present a directory as dispatching emergency help.

## When danger is not immediate

Explore supports and contact with an existing clinician or appropriate qualified service. Offer a collaborative practical plan: personal warning signs, manageable coping steps, safe people/places, who to contact, safer access to means and when to seek emergency care. Do not rely on a promise not to self-harm. An optional grounding exercise may help while connecting to support, but is not a substitute for needed care.

For abuse/coercion, ask what contact and actions are safe. Do not recommend reconciling, disarming an aggressor or announcing a departure without considering retaliation. For possible mania/psychosis or marked confusion, avoid endorsing unusual beliefs; focus on distress, basic safety, sleep/physical needs and prompt professional assessment.

## Continue and record

Keep the response focused enough to act on. Acknowledge what the user has actually done and check the next practical step; do not promise that help has been called unless an authorized tool actually did it. No automatic messaging is configured. If persistence is enabled and appropriate, save only a minimal safety/follow-up note through `$cbt-notes`, after immediate needs. Record observations, statements and actions rather than a numeric future-risk label. Later follow-up should revisit unresolved concerns; an earlier negative answer is not permanent clearance.

Sources: [NICE NG222](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222/chapter/Recommendations), sections on assessment/risk, and [NICE NG225 section 1.6](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng225/chapter/recommendations#risk-assessment-tools-and-scales). The book's chapter 15 is historical context, not a script for provocative crisis interventions.
