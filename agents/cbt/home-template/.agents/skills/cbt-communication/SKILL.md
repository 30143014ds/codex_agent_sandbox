# Criticism, requests and boundaries

Establish the situation, relationship/power context, desired outcome and safety. Do not assume every conflict can be solved by better wording. With abuse, coercion or credible retaliation, prioritize protection, support and feasible boundaries rather than rehearsing a confrontation.

Use [practice.md](references/practice.md) to choose inquiry, acknowledgement of a true point, assertive disagreement, a request, a refusal or negotiation. The user may agree with part of criticism without accepting a false accusation or a global label. A boundary does not require the other person's permission.

For role-play, agree on the role and level of difficulty first. Keep the first exchange short, pause for feedback, and adjust. Do not invent humiliating personal attacks or keep escalating because the user requested directness. Switching roles can reveal assumptions; the fictional response is not evidence of what the real person will do.

Close with a phrase the user can actually say, a safe next step and a fallback. Save a practice/experiment note if useful, preserving uncertainty about the other person's reaction.

Sources: Burns chapters 6–8 and 11; selected Five Secrets principles from later Burns work; CCI assertiveness/criticism materials. Later methods are supplements, not all present as a package in the supplied book.
