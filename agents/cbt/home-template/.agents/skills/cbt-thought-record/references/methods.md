# Thought-work methods

## Burns' ten categories

1. **All-or-nothing thinking:** absolute categories obscure degrees or a mixed result.
2. **Overgeneralization:** one or a few events are taken as a universal pattern.
3. **Mental filter:** selective attention excludes relevant parts of the picture.
4. **Disqualifying the positive:** relevant positive evidence is dismissed by a different standard.
5. **Jumping to conclusions:** an inference exceeds available evidence; subtypes are mind reading and fortune telling.
6. **Magnification/minimization:** importance, probability or consequences are expanded/shrunk without adequate support.
7. **Emotional reasoning:** a feeling is treated as sufficient proof of an external fact or identity.
8. **Should statements:** inflexible demands obscure preferences, constraints or real choices. An ethical obligation is not automatically distorted.
9. **Labeling/mislabeling:** a global identity label substitutes for a specific behavior and context.
10. **Personalization:** responsibility is assigned to the self without fairly considering causation, influence and other contributors.

Mind reading and fortune telling are subtypes of category 5, not replacement categories. Other-blame may be relevant in a contemporary formulation; do not silently present a different taxonomy as the book's original ten.

## Select an alternative angle

- **Define the claim:** what exactly would count as failure, rejection or inadequacy? Replace a global label with observable behavior.
- **Continuum:** identify meaningful degrees between total success and total failure; place this event using explicit criteria.
- **Double standard:** compare the standard used for oneself with the standard used for a respected friend. Do not assume the friend deserves a free pass; seek a fair common standard.
- **Reattribution:** distinguish contribution, intent, control and hindsight; list other causal factors.
- **Semantic change:** translate an absolute demand into a preference, responsibility or practical choice, retaining legitimate values.
- **Cost/benefit:** ask what a thought/rule helps protect and what it costs. Usefulness alone does not establish truth.
- **Test the prediction:** write a concrete prediction, an observable low-risk action and a result that would count either way. Route perfectionism experiments to `$cbt-core-beliefs` and avoidance/pleasure tests to `$cbt-activation`.
- **Brief role reversal:** let the user advise a friend or answer a fictional critic at an agreed intensity. Confirm willingness and stop if it becomes shaming or overwhelming.

For mixed evidence, use a statement such as: “This part happened; this wider conclusion is uncertain; here is what I can do.” If a balanced response feels false, investigate the mismatch rather than insisting repetition will make it true. Record only ratings supplied by the user.
